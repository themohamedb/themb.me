Nidaamka naqshadda ee adeegyada dijitaalka Soomaaliyeed.

An open design system for Somali digital products — names in three parts, money in mobile wallets, phones on uneven networks, families at home and abroad. Built once, so every Somali team gets it right by default.

01

Mabaadi'daPrinciples

Somali users are different, so Hiddo encodes the differences once. Hiddo comes from hiddo iyo dhaqan — heritage and culture.

  1. 01

    Af-Soomaali marka hore

    Somali first

    Design and test in Somali. English is the translation.

  2. 02

    Qof walba

    Everyone

    Works on 2G, an older phone and for low-literacy readers: labels on icons, 16px body, 48px targets.

  3. 03

    Lacag iyo kalsooni

    Money and trust

    Wallet-first. No optimistic UI for money. The recipient's name is always shown before sending.

  4. 04

    Dhaqanku ma aha qurxin

    Culture is structure, not decoration

    Heritage shapes colour, rhythm and a few motifs. It never fills empty space.

  5. 05

    Gudaha iyo dibadda

    Home and abroad

    Same flow in Muqdisho and Minneapolis: country codes, currencies, time zones, languages.

  6. 06

    Furan

    Open

    Free for every Somali company, ministry, NGO and startup to use and extend.

02

FarsamadaCarved and raised

Hiddo surfaces behave like the coral-stone houses of Xamar Weyne: what you type into is carved into the wall, what you press is raised out of it. Every carved edge keeps a 3:1 border, so fields stay findable in bright sun.

Carved · fields

bg-field + inset shadow + border-strong. Focus lifts the field and adds a soft ocean halo.

Raised · actions

bg-raised + shadow-raised. The switch thumb carries a saffron diamond; radio marks are lattice diamonds.

Xeerarka naxwaha · Grammar rules for UI copy

  • *Subject case. A subject before a verb takes its subject form: Xogtani ma aha…, Khadka internetku waa daciif (the marker goes on the last noun), not Xogtan ma aha…*.
  • *No string-gluing. Somali adds suffixes for gender, definiteness and person (akoonka → akoonkaaga*). Write whole sentences per case; never build them as "{noun} + {verb}".
  • *Questions to the user use Ma …? (Ma doonaysaa inaad…?*), not an imperative with a question mark.
  • *Write ma aha** as two words. The apostrophe marks a real glottal stop (la'aan, mabaadi'da, ba'an*), never a contraction.
  • *Second person stays singular in interfaces: aad, -kaaga, ku nooshahay*.
